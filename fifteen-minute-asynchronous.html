Plan 4 of 5 · Two people; one candidate and one reviewer, working at different times

15-minute asynchronous voice-feedback exchange

Budget fifteen active minutes for one directed exchange; waiting for a reply is outside that budget.

Run the timetableUse the session sheetGive bounded feedback

When to use this format

Use this plan when two peers cannot meet at the same time. One person sends a short answer and the other returns bounded feedback; the candidate then makes one revision. The fifteen minutes are the sum of active work stages across this one exchange, not a promise that a reply will arrive within fifteen wall-clock minutes and not fifteen minutes for each person. Agree a realistic calendar deadline separately. To give both people candidate practice, run a second exchange with roles reversed; that requires another fifteen-minute active budget.

Roles, materials, and the opening cue

The candidate chooses the question, owns the answer, and decides whether voice or text is acceptable. The reviewer listens or reads for one chosen focus and returns a small observation. There is no group channel, automatic upload, or default permission to forward the material. Use paper, a local voice recorder already under the candidate’s control, and an exchange method both people already trust. A paper handover or a text-only exchange is enough; no account is required by this handbook. Do not record another person in the room, include employer secrets, or add identifiable client details merely to make the answer sound realistic.

Before recording, both people explicitly agree to the medium, the named recipient, the feedback focus, the reply deadline, and what each will do with their copy afterward. The candidate’s cue is: “May I send you one answer of at most sixty seconds for feedback on whether my own action is clear? Please do not forward it. Let us delete or return our copies after I acknowledge the feedback.” The reviewer confirms or proposes text instead. This is a practical peer agreement, not a legal consent form or a guarantee that a messaging service erases backups. If agreement is absent, do not send an audio file. Use a blank practice sheet until the medium is settled.

The complete timetable

Time check: 2 + 2 + 2 + 1 + 2 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 15 minutes. These are active work stages; waiting between asynchronous replies is not included.

  1. 0–2 minutes · Agree the exchange

    Both complete the medium, recipient, purpose, deadline, and retention agreement. Count only active reading/writing time; pause the practice clock while waiting for confirmation.

  2. 2–4 minutes · Candidate outlines the answer

    Write the question and three factual reminders. Remove sensitive names and details. Choose one listener focus, such as identifying the candidate’s action.

  3. 4–6 minutes · Candidate records or writes

    Make a voice clip no longer than sixty seconds and check it once, or write at most 120 words. Use the remaining stage time to confirm the file or text contains only intended material.

  4. 6–7 minutes · Candidate sends the agreed material

    Include the question and focus with the answer. Use only the agreed recipient and method. The exchange waits here until the reviewer is available.

  5. 7–9 minutes · Reviewer listens or reads twice

    For a sixty-second clip, two listens use the two-minute block. For text, read once for meaning and once for the chosen focus. Do not infer voice pacing from text.

  6. 9–12 minutes · Reviewer sends bounded feedback

    Write one observation, one interpretation to confirm, and one suggested retry. Add a timestamp for voice or a short copied phrase for text when it helps locate the moment.

  7. 12–14 minutes · Candidate reviews and revises

    Read the feedback, correct a mistaken interpretation if needed, and rewrite one sentence. Sending another full recording is outside this exchange’s budget.

  8. 14–15 minutes · Close the exchange

    Candidate acknowledges receipt and both confirm the agreed handling of their copies. If a reply is still missing, close with “not received” rather than claiming the revision was tested.

A small fictional example

Fictional example: a candidate sends a fifty-second account of updating a volunteer rota. The reviewer hears the team’s problem and result but cannot identify who contacted the volunteers. The reviewer points to the middle of the clip and describes that gap without inventing a verbatim transcript. The candidate replies that they made the calls, then rewrites one sentence to say so. The second sentence is a proposed revision only; because it was not recorded and reviewed again, the exchange does not claim that spoken delivery improved.

Feedback prompts and a useful example

The reviewer asks: “What did I understand the candidate to do?”, “Which point am I unsure I heard correctly?”, and “What small revision could clarify that point?” Useful feedback is: “Around the middle of the clip, I understood that the rota changed but could not tell who contacted the volunteers. That is my interpretation, not an exact quote. In a retry, name who made the calls before describing the updated rota.” If using text, quote a short exact passage and explain its effect on understanding. Avoid diagnosing confidence from a voice, treating accent as competence, or assuming a speech-recognition transcript is an accurate record of the audio.

If the session goes off plan

If the clip is longer than sixty seconds, return it with a request for a shorter version before starting the review budget; do not pretend two complete listens fit in two minutes. If the audio is hard to hear, request the text alternative rather than guessing at missing words. If the deadline passes, mark feedback pending or close the exchange; silence is not agreement with the answer. If either person is uncomfortable with the transfer method, use paper or postpone. If sensitive information was sent accidentally, stop further sharing, ask the recipient to follow the agreed deletion or return procedure, and avoid claiming control over copies you cannot inspect.

Debrief and constraints

The candidate records whether the reviewer understood the intended action and which suggestion they accepted. The reviewer records whether the narrow focus made the short exchange manageable. Confirm the copy-handling agreement explicitly, while recognising that you cannot promise deletion from third-party systems. No automatic transcription is needed. The text alternative supports content feedback but cannot test audible pacing or articulation. This exchange checks one answer through one person’s understanding; it does not provide continuous interview practice, a language assessment, or evidence that an asynchronous answer will work in a live conversation.

Printable session sheet

Use paper, or type short notes into these temporary boxes and use your browser’s Print command. This page sends and stores no answers. Reloading or closing may discard typed notes. Keep notes inside the visible boxes when printing; copy anything you want to retain first. Mark uncertain wording as a paraphrase.
